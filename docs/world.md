# Terrain, plants and water

- Plants (`src/world/plants.ts`) are drawn, not simulated, apart from a thin solid capsule at the stem of each big plant (`solid` kinds: fern, Lecocarpus, cotton, tomato; collision group `PLANT_STEM_GROUP`; ground, climb and camera queries use `IGNORE_STEMS` to skip it). Small plants (grass, carpetweed, morning glory, Tiquilia) have no stem: the lizard walks straight through them and presses them flat under its body. Solid stems keep 5 cm apart so the lizard fits between; other plants only 2 cm. Big plants are scattered first and small ones (`SMALL` times as many tries) fill in round them. Plants are also filed in 25 cm cells (`near`) so per-step lookups only visit plants nearby. Tests that need open ground clear a strip with hook `clearPlants` rather than searching for a gap. They slow the lizard through `PlayerController.speedScale`, and each is an overdamped CPU spring (`plantSpring.ts`) pushed by spheres on the drawn lizard and the camera, drawn as instanced meshes bent in the vertex shader (`src/render/plantBend.ts`). Meshes come from `assets-src/plants.py`. The kinds are Galapagos plants, placed by zone: native bunchgrass (`grass`), the endemic Lecocarpus, Darwin's cotton and Galapagos tomato in the dry clearing, ferns only on the Scalesia forest floor, Galapagos carpetweed (`sesuvium`) in the clearing and at the back of the beach, beach morning glory (`ipomoea`) on the open sand and Tiquilia on the bare lava. No meadow flowers (the old daisies and poppies aren't found there). A plant can also be trampled (laid flat, its stem off, standing back up over about a minute), eaten (removed) or sprouted as a seedling that grows in; sprouts draw from a spare instanced patch per kind.

- The world is an 8 m Galapagos-style island (`src/world/layout.ts`): a clearing at the spawn, forest to the west and north, and the coast to the east (`shore.ts`): a sandy beach in the middle, black lava at both ends, the sea at `WATER_Y` (the old pond is gone; `waterDepth`/`inOcean` say where water is). Trees, fallen logs, lava rocks and lava cactus come from `assets-src/props.py` (low poly with natural irregularity: forking limbs ending in leaf `clump`s, rough bark, fractured boulders, ragged log ends; see docs/dunes.md for the techniques) and are scattered deterministically by `src/world/props.ts`. The trees' trunk radii and the cactus's height are pinned (`TRUNKS`, `CACTUS_HEIGHT` in props.py) because everything scattered after them is spaced by those colliders; changing them moves the whole layout. The boulders are the same fractured `rock` as the dunes' basalt; the iguana, feeding and hawk scenarios that pass near them steer around rocks rather than relying on their exact shapes. Each is an `Obstacle` (convex-hull or cylinder collider, faded by the camera when in the way). Lava cactus trunks are in `CACTUS_GROUP`, which the other iguanas don't climb onto and whose tops prick the lizard (docs/player.md). Rocks the tests rely on (`rock-big`, `rock-mid`, `pebble`, `log`, `rock-sunk`, `rock-island`) are placed by name in `obstacles.ts`. Two rock piles (`ROCK_PILES`, built by `rockPiles.ts` from the props' flat-topped `slab_*` meshes) run from the lava out into the sea: stacked slabs, each its own obstacle, with a spine of level steps no higher than the lizard can climb, from the land or out of the water. At the land end of each pile, one shelter to a side, a broad slab lies across two low ones, leaving a gap the lizard can stand in out of sight of anything overhead (`pileShelters()`, `SHELTER`; the loose flank slabs that would fill one are left out, after drawing the same random numbers so every other slab is unchanged). Hook `shelters()`.

- The island's way out (`src/island/`): its objective is to cross the grieta, a lava crack 28 cm wide running wall to wall down the west side, its sheer walls 26 cm deep and its floor sloping in another 10 cm to a slot down the middle (`crack.ts`, cut into the ground by `islandHeight`; `plantingHeight` is the ground before it, so the plants keep their places and random draws). It's too wide to jump. A lizard that falls in slides into the slot and is stuck: "Stuck!" over the screen (`hud.banner`), crawling at 30% speed, the camera swung overhead (`FollowCamera.overhead`) for 2.5 s, then "Climbing back out…" for 1.2 s, and it's put back on the rim it went in from (`run.ts`, `IslandRun`, hook `islandRun().fallen`). Reaching the far side doesn't count while it's down there. The props in the crack, on the trail from the tortoise's meadow to the dead tree, on the tree's fall line and round the fig tree are removed after everything is laid out (`clearForPuzzle`), so every other prop stays put; the plants, algae and reef are laid out round the full list. A wild fig at the forest edge (`fig_tree` in props.glb, made in `assets-src/props.py` like the other trees, from its own random generator so the other props don't change: smooth pale grey bark, a short fluted trunk on buttress roots, low sinuous limbs, a dense dome of leaves, figs on the limbs; planted by `figs.ts`, solid up to its fork) has figs lying under it and drops a new one every 25 to 40 s while fewer than four are about. Figs (yellow, with a stem and an eye so you can see them turn) are drawn, not simulated: they roll as they go, a radian per radius; the lizard's snout noses one along in front of it, its body pushes one aside, one stops against trunks and rocks, and one pushed over the rim drops down the crack, rolls down its floor into the slot and is lost. The dead tree (`snag.ts`) leans out over the crack from its east rim; the tortoise's mouth or shell coming near its foot knocks it over (a rod falling on its foot, on a kinematic body) and it comes to rest across the crack as a log bridge. Over on the far side the run ends with the end screen (`runKeys`, R reloads, C goes on). Going on, the lizard walks itself west up to the bank top (`walkOut.ts`, like the dunes' `Arrival`), the camera swinging round to look out and down, and the screen fades to black before the lagoon loads. Below the bank a mangrove lagoon opens out past the west wall (`basin.ts`): the ground drops under the water there and a gap is cut in the bank (kept above the water, so no plant or prop moves). Backdrop trees standing in it are dropped. Red mangroves stand in still water and black mangroves on the banks (`lagoonView.ts`, the lagoon's own lagoon.glb, its own random numbers), drawn only once the lizard is over. The way on from the bridge to the bank top is cleared like the trail. Hooks `islandLeave()` and `islandWalkOut()`. The objective line over the goals gives a hint for the step reached (`IslandRun.objective`), and a card at the start says what each bar is. Hooks `figs()`, `figPlace()`, `figTree()`, `snag()`, `knockSnag()`, `crack(z)`, `islandRun()`.

- Algae (`src/world/algae.ts`) are drawn, not simulated: a registry of patches by id. `bite(id, share)` shrinks a patch and the last bite removes it (`remove(id)`); every 10 to 30 s a new one sprouts on a bare site (spare sites seeded on the same rocks, or where one was eaten) and grows in over 40 s. Each instanced batch has room for all its tile's sites, so a sprout never needs a new mesh. Hooks `algae()` (with `grown`), `removeAlgae(id)`, `sproutAlgae()`.

- The sea is zoned by depth (`SEA_ZONES` in `shore.ts`): algae in the shallows, thinning out from 12 to 22 cm down, and coral from 14 cm, as thick as it gets by 24 cm, so the two mix in between. Algae sites below the shallows are dropped after they're drawn, by a hash of the spot, so the shallow patches are exactly where they were. Corals, urchins and sea stars (`src/world/reef.ts`, models in `assets-src/reef.py` → `reef.glb`) are drawn, not simulated, like the algae: no colliders, the lizard swims through them. Mostly over the rocky bottom off the lava and a little off the beach: cauliflower coral (Pocillopora) bushes, lobe coral (Porites) domes, sea fans (tinted per fan, broadside to the swell, swaying in the vertex shader) and orange sun corals at the foot of sunken boulders, all in the coral zone; pencil urchins and Panamic cushion stars at any depth, on the bottom and on rock tops. Always wholly under the surface and clear of the algae. They draw from their own random numbers, so adding them moved nothing else. Hook `corals()`.

- `SeaBed` (`src/world/seaBed.ts`) is a grid, made once at load, of the height of whatever is under the water: the sea floor, or a rock taken as a dome over its footprint. Drawn swimmers (the fish) use it to keep off the bottom and out of the rocks without casting rays.
