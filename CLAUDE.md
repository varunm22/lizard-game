# Lizard Game

Toon-styled 3D browser game: a small lizard exploring a miniature natural world.
Three.js + TypeScript for rendering, Rapier (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) for physics, Vite for builds.

## Commands

- `npm run dev` — dev server
- `npm run typecheck` — TypeScript check
- `npm run build` — typecheck + production build to `dist/`
- `npm run assets` — regenerate GLB models from `assets-src/`
- `npm test` — Playwright: builds, serves, boots the game in headless Chromium, saves screenshots to `test-results/screenshots/`

In Claude cloud sessions Chromium is preinstalled; do not run `playwright install`.

## Conventions

- World units are metres at real scale: the lizard is ~0.19 m long, snout to tail tip. Keep camera near-plane and fog tuned for that.
- Physics is the source of truth. Rapier steps at a fixed 60 Hz in `src/loop.ts`; meshes only follow bodies and interpolate between steps.
- Gravity is always straight down. No wall/ceiling traversal or full runtime foot IK (out of scope for V1); the asset script uses IK only to bake planted feet into the clips. At runtime the drawn lizard is fitted to the surface instead: the spine bends to follow it (`src/player/spineFit.ts`) each leg swings about the body's long axis to plant its foot (`src/player/legReach.ts`), and last, tail bones and legs are turned out of any rock, log or ground they'd sit inside (`src/player/bodyClearance.ts`; hook `clearance()`). The physics capsule stays straight and the tail has no collider.
- Keep systems as plain TypeScript modules, one concern per file. No ECS framework or engine layer.
- Expose anything a test needs to check on `window.__game` (`src/debug/testHooks.ts`) rather than testing pixels.
- Models are generated by Blender Python scripts in `assets-src/` and exported as GLB to `src/assets/` (committed). Run `npm run assets` after editing a script; it needs `pip install -r assets-src/requirements.txt` (the `bpy` package is Blender as a Python module, Python 3.11 only).
- Plants (`src/world/plants.ts`) are drawn, not simulated, apart from a thin solid capsule at each stem (collision group `PLANT_STEM_GROUP`; ground, climb and camera queries use `IGNORE_STEMS` to skip it). They slow the lizard through `PlayerController.speedScale`, and each is an overdamped CPU spring (`plantSpring.ts`) pushed by spheres on the drawn lizard and the camera, drawn as instanced meshes bent in the vertex shader (`src/render/plantBend.ts`). Meshes come from `assets-src/plants.py`. A plant can also be trampled (laid flat, its stem off, standing back up over about a minute), eaten (removed) or sprouted as a seedling that grows in; sprouts draw from a spare instanced patch per kind.
- The world is an 8 m Galapagos-style island (`src/world/layout.ts`): a clearing at the spawn, forest to the west and north, and the coast to the east (`shore.ts`): a sandy beach in the middle, black lava at both ends, the sea at `WATER_Y` (the old pond is gone; `waterDepth`/`inOcean` say where water is). Trees, fallen logs, lava rocks and lava cactus come from `assets-src/props.py` and are scattered deterministically by `src/world/props.ts`; each is an `Obstacle` (convex-hull or cylinder collider, faded by the camera when in the way). Rocks the tests rely on (`rock-big`, `rock-mid`, `pebble`, `log`, `rock-sunk`, `rock-island`) are placed by name in `obstacles.ts`.
- Algae (`src/world/algae.ts`) are drawn, not simulated: a registry of patches by id, each removable with `Algae.remove(id)` (hooks `algae()` / `removeAlgae(id)`), ready for an eating mechanic.
- The player is drawn as a marine iguana, generated by `assets-src/lizard.py`: proportions measured off side-on photos, and a skin texture painted with numpy (one JPEG inside the GLB). The physics capsule covers snout to vent. Rig points with no joint (snout, tail tip, feet) are glTF extras on the armature, read by `src/player/lizardModel.ts`.
- The lizard faces +Z in game space. Its clips are idle, walk, run, jump, fall, land, swim; gait clip speeds live in `LIZARD_GAIT_SPEED` and must match the script.
- Other animals live in `src/creatures/`, one file each. The giant tortoise (`assets-src/tortoise.py`, faces -Y in Blender like the lizard) walks the oval `TORTOISE_ROUTE` (`src/world/layout.ts`) on a kinematic body, fitted to the ground under its feet. Its convex shell collider is built from carapace points the script exports (`shell_hull` extra) and rises and falls with the drawn shell's body bone, so the lizard can ride it; a lizard standing on it is carried along (`PlayerController.carry`). It tramples plants under it, sometimes stops to eat the one at its mouth, rests now and then, and shoves the lizard aside with `PlayerController.shove`. `Regrowth` (`src/creatures/route.ts`) keeps a meadow along the route and sprouts seedlings on it.
