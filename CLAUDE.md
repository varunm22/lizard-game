# Lizard Game

Toon-styled 3D browser game: a small lizard exploring a miniature natural world.
Three.js + TypeScript for rendering, Rapier (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) for physics, Vite for builds.

## Commands

- `npm run dev` — dev server
- `npm run typecheck` — TypeScript check
- `npm run build` — typecheck + production build to `dist/`
- `npm run assets` — regenerate GLB models from `assets-src/`
- `npm test` — Playwright: builds, serves, boots the game in headless Chromium, saves screenshots to `test-results/screenshots/`

In Claude cloud sessions Chromium is preinstalled; do not run `playwright install`.

## Conventions

- World units are metres at real scale: the lizard is ~0.19 m long, snout to tail tip. Keep camera near-plane and fog tuned for that.
- Physics is the source of truth. Rapier steps at a fixed 60 Hz in `src/loop.ts`; meshes only follow bodies and interpolate between steps.
- Gravity is always straight down. No wall/ceiling traversal or full runtime foot IK (out of scope for V1); the asset script uses IK only to bake planted feet into the clips. At runtime the drawn lizard is fitted to the surface instead: the spine bends to follow it (`src/player/spineFit.ts`) each leg swings about the body's long axis to plant its foot (`src/player/legReach.ts`), and last, tail bones and legs are turned out of any rock, log or ground they'd sit inside (`src/player/bodyClearance.ts`; hooks `clearance()`, `clearancePoints()`), gently: grazes are left alone and turns ease in and out. Those fitting queries see the tortoise shell through `src/world/exactSurface.ts`, exactly and where it's drawn this frame, because Rapier's queries on a convex hull this small are off by millimetres. Three's mixer only writes a bone when its clip value changes, so `LizardModel.update` restores every bone to its last clip pose before mixing; never reset bones to rest there. Rapier's character controller can jam on a top it rests on (an iguana's back) or crawl on some gentle ground; `PlayerController` retries such a move without sinking, then lifted 0.5 mm. Hook `frame(dt)` plays one display frame (steps plus interpolation) for checking jitter. The physics capsule stays straight and the tail has no collider.
- Keep systems as plain TypeScript modules, one concern per file. No ECS framework or engine layer.
- Expose anything a test needs to check on `window.__game` (`src/debug/testHooks.ts`) rather than testing pixels.
- Models are generated by Blender Python scripts in `assets-src/` and exported as GLB to `src/assets/` (committed). Run `npm run assets` after editing a script; it needs `pip install -r assets-src/requirements.txt` (the `bpy` package is Blender as a Python module, Python 3.11 only).
- Plants (`src/world/plants.ts`) are drawn, not simulated, apart from a thin solid capsule at each stem (collision group `PLANT_STEM_GROUP`; ground, climb and camera queries use `IGNORE_STEMS` to skip it). They slow the lizard through `PlayerController.speedScale`, and each is an overdamped CPU spring (`plantSpring.ts`) pushed by spheres on the drawn lizard and the camera, drawn as instanced meshes bent in the vertex shader (`src/render/plantBend.ts`). Meshes come from `assets-src/plants.py`. A plant can also be trampled (laid flat, its stem off, standing back up over about a minute), eaten (removed) or sprouted as a seedling that grows in; sprouts draw from a spare instanced patch per kind.
- The world is an 8 m Galapagos-style island (`src/world/layout.ts`): a clearing at the spawn, forest to the west and north, and the coast to the east (`shore.ts`): a sandy beach in the middle, black lava at both ends, the sea at `WATER_Y` (the old pond is gone; `waterDepth`/`inOcean` say where water is). Trees, fallen logs, lava rocks and lava cactus come from `assets-src/props.py` and are scattered deterministically by `src/world/props.ts`; each is an `Obstacle` (convex-hull or cylinder collider, faded by the camera when in the way). Rocks the tests rely on (`rock-big`, `rock-mid`, `pebble`, `log`, `rock-sunk`, `rock-island`) are placed by name in `obstacles.ts`. Two rock piles (`ROCK_PILES`, built by `rockPiles.ts` from the props' flat-topped `slab_*` meshes) run from the lava out into the sea: stacked slabs, each its own obstacle, with a spine of level steps no higher than the lizard can climb, from the land or out of the water.
- Algae (`src/world/algae.ts`) are drawn, not simulated: a registry of patches by id. `bite(id, share)` shrinks a patch and the last bite removes it (`remove(id)`); every 10 to 30 s a new one sprouts on a bare site (spare sites seeded on the same rocks, or where one was eaten) and grows in over 40 s. Each instanced batch has room for all its tile's sites, so a sprout never needs a new mesh. Hooks `algae()` (with `grown`), `removeAlgae(id)`, `sproutAlgae()`.
- The player is drawn as a marine iguana, generated by `assets-src/lizard.py`: proportions measured off side-on photos, and a skin texture painted with numpy (one JPEG inside the GLB). The physics capsule covers snout to vent. Rig points with no joint (snout, tail tip, feet) are glTF extras on the armature, read by `src/player/lizardModel.ts`.
- The lizard faces +Z in game space. Its clips are idle, walk, run, jump, fall, land, swim; gait clip speeds live in `LIZARD_GAIT_SPEED` and must match the script. A `bite` clip (neck, head and a `jaw` bone; the head mesh is split along the lips, with a palate and mouth floor inside) is layered additively over whichever clip is playing: `LizardModel.bite(onSnap)` plays it and calls back as the jaws close (`BITE_SNAP` must match the script). F (gamepad X) bites (`src/player/feeding.ts`): algae touching the snout or head as it snaps get `Algae.bite` (`Algae.touching` finds them); grazing iguanas bite with the same clip. Hooks `bite()`, `feeding()`, `lizard().jawOpen`.
- Other animals live in `src/creatures/`, one file each. The giant tortoise (`assets-src/tortoise.py`, faces -Y in Blender like the lizard) walks the oval `TORTOISE_ROUTE` (`src/world/layout.ts`) on a kinematic body, fitted to the ground under its feet. Its convex shell collider is built from carapace points the script exports (`shell_hull` extra) and rises and falls with the drawn shell's body bone, so the lizard can ride it; a lizard standing on it is carried along (`PlayerController.carry`). It tramples plants under it, sometimes stops to eat the one at its mouth, rests now and then, and shoves the lizard aside with `PlayerController.shove`. `Regrowth` (`src/creatures/route.ts`) keeps a meadow along the route and sprouts seedlings on it.
- Other marine iguanas (`src/creatures/iguana.ts`): three on the lava shore, each the player's own lizard (a `PlayerController`, `MovementStateMachine`, `LizardModel` and `LizardVisual`) driven by its own `InputState` instead of the keyboard, tinted per iguana. They bask on black lava only (`lavaCover` ≥ 0.85), broadside to the sun, or alongside a lizard (the player or another iguana) already lying still on lava nearby, walking in along its line. They sneeze salt (`saltSpray.ts`, head nod via `LizardModel.nod`), and when hungry walk into the sea, swim and dive to a grown algae patch, nose in until the drawn snout is in its fronds, bite it down and eat it, maybe go on to another, and come back out to bask; they keep off the rock piles. A body in their way they walk round by a point beside it, keeping to the side they picked, and they come in to lie beside a mate along a line off its end, lying down once roughly level with it and near the line (`MATE_LEVEL`, `MATE_LINE`) rather than pushing on for the exact spot; they give way rather than push. The lizard walking into one pushes it slowly aside (`Iguanas.pushedBy`, from `PlayerController.blockers`), and the camera's arm passes through them. Bodies are solid, in `IGUANA_GROUP`; crabs' rays and the `groundAt` hook look past them (`IGNORE_STEMS_AND_IGUANAS`). A still lizard is stepped with `PlayerController.stillGroups` so Rapier's character controller can't let it creep beside a moving body. Hooks `iguanas()`, `iguanaDo(i, 'feed' | 'move' | 'sneeze')`, `iguanaPlace(i, x, z, yaw)`, `saltSpray()`.
- Sally Lightfoot crabs (`src/creatures/crab.ts`, model `assets-src/crab.py`, faces -Y in Blender, +X its left) live on the lava shore and the rock piles. They're drawn, not simulated: no collider, kept on the surface by downward rays that ignore the lizard. Each walks sideways round its home, grazes (at algae above the water when there's some), hops any step from 5 mm up to 8 cm (the hop clips stay in place; the game carries it through the air), runs from the lizard and ducks if cornered, and never goes below `WATER_Y`. A crab under the lizard's body is pushed out from under it, and crabs keep a leg span apart. There are 18 of them. Once the lizard has lain still for 4 s crabs stop fearing it, and the nearest one with a clear way walks over, hops onto its back (`LizardModel.back`) and grooms it with the graze clip until the lizard moves; any still iguana is a `Host` they groom the same way (hook `crabs()` says whom with `grooming`). Crabs fear any iguana that isn't calm (the player or another, up and about or not yet still for 4 s; the others start calm), and a crab that gets no nearer where it's going for a second gives up. Rays onto a rock's convex hull are a few mm noisy, so tests check crab heights to within a few mm.
